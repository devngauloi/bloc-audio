25/5 pomodoro —
the block you finish is the only one that counts

Most advice about the 25/5 ratio argues about the 25. The more useful question is what happens at minute 24 when something interrupts you, because that is where the difference between a good session and a wasted one actually lives.

25 minutes of work, 5 minutes of break. It is the shortest of the common ratios and the one people reach for first, usually because starting is the hard part and 25 is the number that sounds survivable. That is the right instinct, and it is worth being precise about why.

An abandoned block leaves nothing behind

A finished 25-minute block and a 50-minute block abandoned at 30 are not "25 minutes" and "30 minutes" of work. The finished one ends with a stopping point you chose, a note about what comes next, and the small fact that you did the thing you said you would. The abandoned one ends mid-sentence and leaves you knowing that sitting down did not work, which is the belief that makes tomorrow harder.

So the rule is: pick the longest block you can finish on a bad day, not on a good one. Good days do not need help. Save 50/10 and 90/15 for the days that are already going well — the longer ratios have their own requirements, and one of them is that you can protect the time.

What a daily minute quota does to a timer, and why we removed ours

This is the part we got wrong ourselves, so it is worth writing down.

The first version of our free tier was a quota: 30 minutes of timer per day. It sounds generous and neutral. In practice the app offers four session lengths, and a 25/5 session runs 30 minutes end to end. So the quota fitted exactly one of the four, and the default was not that one. Every longer session started normally, ran, and then stopped partway through when the quota ran out.

Session Runs Under a 30-minute daily quota
25/5 30 min fits, barely
50/10 60 min cut at 30
90/15 105 min cut at 30

Three of the four were designed to be interrupted — by us, on purpose, as the monetisation. An app sold on protecting a block of focus was cutting the block in half. Nobody complained; it simply looked like a pricing decision on a spreadsheet.

What replaced it: one full session a day, free, whichever length you choose, and it always runs to the end. A day is only spent when a block reaches zero — ending early costs nothing, so there is never a reason to sit through a session you have stopped needing. If you want a second session, or a longer one, that is what the paid tier is for, and the price is on the screen before you start rather than after.

The general form of this, useful when judging any freemium app: ask what the free tier interrupts. A limit on how many times you can do something is a limit. A limit that lands in the middle of the thing is a trap, and it is usually invisible until you hit it.

The arithmetic of 30 minutes of music

If you run 25/5 with audio, the music keeps playing through the break, so one session is 30 continuous minutes, not 25. That is the number the audio has to cover.

Thirty minutes is a low bar, and almost any playlist clears it — which is exactly why the pool size question only becomes interesting at the longer ratios. What still matters at 25/5 is the other two things:

  • Nothing with words. Wordless is the floor, not the finish line: a wordless track can still be too bright to ignore. There is a measurement for this and it is not the genre label.
  • One consistent level. Reaching for the volume at minute 12 is an interruption you inflicted on yourself. A set mastered to a common loudness does not ask you to.

Three taps, and why the count matters more at 25 minutes

The shorter the block, the larger the share of it that setup eats. Choosing a length, choosing a sound, and pressing start are three decisions before any work happens, and they land in the exact window where you were still willing to begin. At 90 minutes that is noise. At 25 minutes it is a real fraction of the session.

Which is why the last preset and the last sound should already be selected when you open a timer app, so starting is one tap rather than three. If yours makes you re-choose every time, that is the thing to fix — before any of the advice about ratios.
