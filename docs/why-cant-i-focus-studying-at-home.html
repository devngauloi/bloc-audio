Why you can't focus while
studying at home

Written by the person who makes Bloc Focus Music. The last section is about the app; the first three are not, and they stand on their own.

Almost everything written about this treats it as a character problem — you lack discipline, you need a better morning routine, you must want it more. That framing is popular because it is easy to write and impossible to disprove.

It is also the wrong place to look first. Focus at home breaks for reasons that are mostly physical and mostly boring, and the boring reasons are the ones you can actually change this afternoon.

What is actually different about home

A library, a lecture hall, and a café all do one thing your bedroom does not: they put you in a place whose only obvious use is the thing you came to do. You do not have to decide to work there. The room has already decided.

At home you are asking one room to be a bedroom, a dining room, a cinema and an office, and asking your attention to referee between them. Three things follow, and each has a cheap fix:

  • No boundary in space. The desk you study at is the desk you scroll at. Every object in reach is a live link to something else.
  • No boundary in time. Nothing starts, so nothing ends. Sessions bleed into a whole evening of half-working, which feels worse and produces less than ninety honest minutes would.
  • Interruption is free. In a library, checking your phone has a small social cost. At home it has none, so it happens at the first moment the work gets hard — which is exactly the moment the work started being useful.

Notice that none of these three is about willpower. They are all about the room and the clock.

What to try first, ordered by what it costs you

In that order. Do not skip to the bottom because the bottom is more fun to buy.

Cost Try this What it fixes
Free Sit somewhere else. A different room, the kitchen table, a chair that faces a wall instead of a bed. Anywhere your body has not already learned to relax. The missing boundary in space
Free Put the phone in another room — not face-down, not on silent, physically elsewhere. The distance is the whole mechanism; a phone within arm's reach still costs attention even when it never lights up. Interruption being free
Free Decide the stopping time before you start, out loud or on paper. "Until 4:30" beats "for a while" by a wide margin, because a session with no end is a session you can quietly abandon without noticing. The missing boundary in time

If the first three work, you are done, and you have spent nothing. That is a real and common outcome, and it is not in anybody's interest to tell you about it — which is roughly the point of the next section.

Something we measured, that argues against our own product

On 29 August 2026 we ran a demand check on what people actually type when they look for this. Not a survey — just the completions a search box offers, which are ranked by real query volume even though the volume itself is not published. Treat it as a proxy, not a measurement.

Two results were not what an app maker wants to find:

  • The most common things people attach to study timer are online, aesthetic, website and for pc. A large share of that demand is not looking for something to install. It is looking for a page that already has a clock on it.
  • In Japan, the same kind of query surfaces ドリテック — a maker of kitchen and study timers — and 100均, the hundred-yen shops. The competition for "help me focus" there is a physical timer that costs about a dollar.

We think that is the honest headline: most people looking for a way to focus do not need another app. They need a boundary, and a boundary can be a closed door, a stopping time, or a plastic timer from a discount shop.

So when is an app worth it

Only when it removes a decision you would otherwise make dozens of times. Concretely, an app earns its place if — and only if — you recognise yourself in one of these:

  • You want music while you work, and picking the music has itself become the distraction. Choosing a playlist is a decision; making that decision fifteen times a session is a leak that a paper timer cannot plug.
  • You need the same session length repeatedly and want it to start in one tap, without re-typing it.
  • Your phone has to stay in the room for a real reason, and you would rather it be running the session than sitting there being a phone.

If none of those describe you, close this page and go move your chair. That advice is free and it works.

What Bloc does, and what it does not

Bloc Focus Music is a focus timer with instrumental study music inside it. It exists for the first case above: one tap starts a session and the music, so there is no playlist to choose and nothing to browse on the way in. Nothing is sold to you while the timer is running, and there are no ads at any point.

The limits, stated here rather than discovered later:

  • Android only. There is no iPhone version yet.
  • The free tier is one full 25/5 session a day, at that one length. It always runs to the end — it is never cut short mid-session.
  • The music library is small compared to a streaming service, and only the bundled tracks play with no connection.
  • The music is AI-generated, instrumental, and licensed for commercial use. We say so on every surface rather than letting you work it out.
